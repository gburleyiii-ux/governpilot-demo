# Health 42 CFR Part 2 Substance Use Disorder Records Standard

42 CFR Part 2 protects records that would identify a patient as having a substance use
disorder when created by a federally assisted SUD program. It is more restrictive than HIPAA:
it has historically required patient consent for disclosures HIPAA permits without
authorization, and it bars use of records in proceedings against the patient.

The 2024 final rule (HHS Office for Civil Rights and SAMHSA, FR Doc 2024-02544, effective
April 16, 2024, compliance date February 16, 2026) aligns Part 2 more closely with HIPAA while
keeping heightened protections. Key changes: a single patient consent authorizes all future
uses and disclosures for treatment, payment, and health care operations until revoked in
writing (§2.31, §2.33); a covered entity or business associate that receives records under
that consent may redisclose them per HIPAA, except for proceedings against the patient
(§2.33); a separate consent or court order is required to use records in any civil, criminal,
administrative, or legislative proceeding against the patient (§§2.63-2.64); each consent-based
disclosure must be accompanied by a copy of the consent or a clear explanation of its scope
(§2.32, Notice to Accompany Disclosure); HIPAA breach notification now applies (§2.16). The
rule removes the mandatory data-segregation requirement for records received under a single
consent (§2.12(d)), but those records remain Part 2 records subject to the proceedings bar, so
provenance must still be tracked. Compliance for the accounting-of-disclosures provision
(§2.25) is tolled pending revision of HIPAA's 45 CFR 164.528.

A control plane should enforce: a valid, non-revoked TPO consent before disclosure; the
accompanying notice on every consent-based disclosure; a block on proceeding use without a
separate consent or court order; and a persistent Part 2 provenance tag so the proceedings bar
travels with the record even when physical segregation is not required.

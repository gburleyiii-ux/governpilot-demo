# Health Utilization Review and Patient AI Disclosure Standard

Prior authorization and coverage decisions. The CMS Interoperability and Prior Authorization
Final Rule (CMS-0057-F, 89 FR 8758) applies to Medicare Advantage organizations, Medicaid
fee-for-service and managed care, CHIP, and Qualified Health Plan issuers on the Federally
Facilitated Exchanges. From January 1, 2026 impacted payers (excluding QHP issuers on the
FFEs, and excluding drug prior authorizations) must send decisions within 72 hours for
expedited requests and seven calendar days for standard requests, must provide a specific
reason for denials, and must publicly report prior-authorization metrics annually (initial
set due March 31, 2026). A Prior Authorization API is required from January 1, 2027.

Algorithms in coverage decisions. Under 42 CFR 422.101(c) and the CMS FAQ of February 6, 2024
(interpreting CMS-4201-F), an algorithm or AI tool may assist a Medicare Advantage plan but
cannot by itself be the basis for an adverse determination. The plan must base medical-necessity
decisions on the individual patient's circumstances — medical history, physician recommendations,
clinical notes — not solely on a group dataset, and a length-of-stay or admission prediction
alone cannot be used to terminate post-acute care or deny an admission. AI cannot shift posted
coverage criteria over time, and plans must guard against bias under Section 1557.

California reinforces this. SB 1120, the Physicians Make Decisions Act (effective January 1,
2025), provides that a determination of medical necessity may be made only by a licensed
physician or qualified licensed professional; an AI tool used in utilization review cannot deny,
delay, or modify services based on medical necessity and cannot base determinations solely on a
group dataset.

Patient-facing AI disclosure. California AB 3030 (effective January 1, 2025) requires a
disclaimer that a communication was generated by generative AI, plus instructions for contacting
a human provider, when generative AI produces patient clinical communications — unless a licensed
provider read and reviewed the communication. Utah's AI Policy Act (as amended) and HB 452
require disclosure that a user is interacting with AI in regulated occupations and mental-health
chatbots, and Texas TRAIGA (HB 149, effective January 1, 2026) requires disclosure of AI
interaction in health care contexts.

A control plane should evidence: a specific denial reason, an individualized-assessment record,
a qualified human decision-maker on adverse determinations, decision timeframes, and a present
patient-facing AI disclosure (or a logged human-review exemption).
